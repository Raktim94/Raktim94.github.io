FIELD NOTE 08 · KINETIRX

Self-Hosting Is Not a Backup Strategy

IDC Frontier reported a ransomware incident affecting part of its cloud service in October 2026 and described impact to 495 organizations. Its later update noted that snapshot availability differed among customers.

By Raktim Ranjit · Updated 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

The incident, and the operational question

The uncomfortable lesson from a provider-side incident is that “our server is in the cloud” says nothing about whether a clean, restorable copy exists. Backups need separate credentials, a separate failure domain, retention, and a recovery test. A snapshot that cannot be reached or restored on time is not a continuity plan.

What the primary record says

IDC Frontier’s October 2026 Japanese-language updates say ransomware affected portions of its East Japan Region 1 service beginning on 7 October, impacting 495 corporate and municipal customers across four zones. In its 14 October update, the provider said backup retrieval was possible for 194 customers using its snapshot function; later updates reported that additional data had been found recoverable. The numbers and recovery picture changed as investigation continued. This is a particularly useful reminder that a provider snapshot is not automatically equivalent to an independently protected, tested backup. Read the provider’s latest notice for current scope and dates before drawing conclusions about individual customers.

What this means for a working business

A self-hosted system still depends on the quality and separation of its backups. If production and snapshots share credentials, storage, administrative access or the same compromised environment, ransomware may affect both. A sound plan identifies the recovery point (how much work might be lost), recovery time (how long service may be unavailable), retention period and who can restore. It also tests that a backup is readable and that the application can use it. ‘We take snapshots’ is a claim about a mechanism; ‘we restored last month’ is evidence about a process.

Where the product fits in the actual workflow

KinetiRx is self-hosted, with a PostgreSQL database on hardware selected by the operator. That gives a pharmacy latitude to choose backup destinations and retention, but it does not make backup copies automatic or ransomware-proof. The operator must configure the database backup, protect credentials, keep a separate copy, monitor failures and rehearse restore. Because the records may support pharmacy workflows, recovery planning also needs a safe manual process and a clear reconciliation owner. Product documentation should be read alongside the pharmacy’s own privacy, retention and security requirements.

Walk through a realistic failure

In a ransomware exercise, assume the production host and its attached snapshot credentials are unavailable. The operator should identify a clean device, a separate backup copy and the trusted steps to restore PostgreSQL or the relevant application data. Verify that the restored data opens and that a sample record, user role and recent transaction are present. Keep the compromised system isolated while the recovery owner decides what can be safely reused. For a pharmacy, staff need an approved downtime workflow during the restore; for any business, the owner should record how much data was lost and which transactions require reconciliation.

A practical continuity drill

Run this during a quiet period with the person who will make the decision during a real incident. Write down what you observe, what is unavailable, and how staff return to normal operation. A drill that ends with a clear owner and a measured recovery time is more useful than a checklist nobody has tried.

  • Keep at least one backup copy isolated from routine production credentials and network access.
  • Set a retention schedule that covers delayed discovery, not just yesterday’s file.
  • Restore into a clean test environment and verify the data, user accounts and application startup.
  • Write down an incident contact tree and a temporary way to operate while recovery is underway.

Questions to answer before relying on the setup

  • Could an attacker using the server account delete every backup?
  • How old will the restored data be after the longest planned outage?
  • Has the team restored onto clean hardware rather than only listing snapshot files?
  • What work can continue safely during the restore window?

Scope and limitations

Self-hosting does not prevent ransomware or make backups automatic. The project should not be treated as a substitute for professional security review, offsite backups, or incident response.

Product documentation to verify before deployment

KinetiRx is self-hosted and places the pharmacy’s database on operator-controlled infrastructure. That means the pharmacy can choose its own backup location and schedule. The responsibility is equally direct: configure backups, protect them from ransomware, and test restoring a database before patient care depends on it.
