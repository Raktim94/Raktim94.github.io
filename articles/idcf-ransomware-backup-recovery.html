FIELD NOTE 08 · KINETIRX

Self-Hosting Is Not a Backup Strategy

IDC Frontier reported a ransomware incident affecting part of its cloud service in October 2026 and described impact to 495 organizations. Its later update noted that snapshot availability differed among customers.

By Raktim Ranjit · 27 October 2026 · 5 min read

The incident, and the question it raises

The uncomfortable lesson from a provider-side incident is that “our server is in the cloud” says nothing about whether a clean, restorable copy exists. Backups need separate credentials, a separate failure domain, retention, and a recovery test. A snapshot that cannot be reached or restored on time is not a continuity plan.

Where KinetiRx fits

KinetiRx is self-hosted and places the pharmacy’s database on operator-controlled infrastructure. That means the pharmacy can choose its own backup location and schedule. The responsibility is equally direct: configure backups, protect them from ransomware, and test restoring a database before patient care depends on it.

A clear boundary

Self-hosting does not prevent ransomware or make backups automatic. The project should not be treated as a substitute for professional security review, offsite backups, or incident response.
