FIELD NOTE 06 · ORDERRESTRO

Remote Access Is a Convenience; the Restaurant LAN Is the Fallback

Cloudflare’s November 2025 postmortem described a global failure to deliver core network traffic. For a restaurant system, a tunnel can help remote access but should not be confused with the local service.

By Raktim Ranjit · 27 October 2026 · 5 min read

The incident, and the question it raises

A tunnel or reverse proxy can make a local service reachable from outside, but it adds another provider and network path. When that layer is down, remote owners may lose access even while staff on the restaurant Wi-Fi can still work. That separation should be rehearsed instead of discovered during dinner service.

Where OrderRestro fits

OrderRestro’s public docs describe both a local-LAN deployment and optional tunnel or reverse-proxy access. The local setup is the natural continuity path for tables, billing, KDS, and printers. Remote access should be treated as a separate capability with its own failure mode.

A clear boundary

A tunnel does not make the app immune to a Cloudflare incident, and local operation does not guarantee that every integration or payment provider works offline.
