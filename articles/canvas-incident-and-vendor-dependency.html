FIELD NOTE 12 · ORDERRESTRO

When a Critical Workflow Lives in One Vendor’s Login Screen

Instructure documented unauthorized Canvas activity in April and May 2026, with the service temporarily placed in maintenance mode during containment. The outage landed during a high-stakes academic period for some users.

By Raktim Ranjit · 27 October 2026 · 5 min read

The incident, and the question it raises

Vendor concentration is not inherently wrong; a focused provider can deliver excellent software. The risk appears when a business has no documented fallback for the workflow that provider owns. A restaurant should decide how it records orders, kitchen tickets, and payments if its application, identity provider, or remote host is unreachable.

Where OrderRestro fits

OrderRestro is designed around a local restaurant server and LAN access for order-taking, billing, kitchen display, and printing. This challenges the assumption that all operational software must be SaaS, while keeping the system operator responsible for maintenance and continuity.

A clear boundary

Local hosting does not make a vendor-free system: hardware, operating systems, payment rails, and the people maintaining it still matter. A manual fallback procedure is still useful.
