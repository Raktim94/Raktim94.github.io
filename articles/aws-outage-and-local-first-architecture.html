The AWS Outage Lesson: Count Dependencies, Not Just Servers

AWS traced its October 2025 US-EAST-1 event to DNS resolution problems for regional DynamoDB endpoints, with recovery continuing after the initial issue was mitigated.

By Raktim Ranjit · 27 October 2026 · 5 min read

The incident, and the question it raises

A business can have healthy application code and still be unable to start, look up, or coordinate services when a shared dependency fails. The useful post-incident exercise is to draw the dependency chain: DNS, database discovery, deployment control plane, identity, edge, payment, and the local network.

Where OrderRestro fits

OrderRestro’s documented local deployment puts the database, API, and web application on a restaurant LAN server. That can shorten the dependency chain for in-house ordering and kitchen work. The system still needs reliable local hardware, tested database backups, and a recovery plan.

A clear boundary

AWS’s incident is not evidence that self-hosted systems are automatically safer. OrderRestro can also be deployed on a VPS; in that mode, provider outages and internet access remain material dependencies.
