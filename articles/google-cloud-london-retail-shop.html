FIELD NOTE 03 · NODEDR POS

A Shop Checkout Should Have a Plan for the Network It Depends On

The October 2026 Google Cloud disruption is a prompt to distinguish a cloud dashboard from the local checkout path a shop needs at the counter.

By Raktim Ranjit · Updated 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

The incident, and the operational question

Regional service issues make a simple design question visible: does scanning an item, updating stock, and producing a receipt require a round trip to a remote service? A small shop may accept cloud dependencies, but it should choose them deliberately and know what happens when they fail.

What the primary record says

The Google Cloud incident in europe-west2 lasted 6 hours and 10 minutes according to the provider’s preliminary report. It began with a power interruption at the europe-west2-b facility; the report describes a failure in the automatic generator start/transfer sequence after UPS power was used. Hosts, routing equipment and storage in that zone went offline, with knock-on errors and queuing in some regional services. That is a useful example of how an individual zone problem can affect a broader service path. It does not show that every cloud application, every checkout, or every region was unavailable.

What this means for a working business

At a retail counter, separate the operations that happen inside the shop from those that must reach an external network. Scanning a barcode, looking up a local item, recording a sale and printing a receipt may be possible on the local network. A hosted server, online catalogue, remote backup, payment gateway or synchronization job may not be. Calling a POS ‘offline-first’ is only useful when staff know exactly which actions are available offline and how to reconcile later. A clear fallback avoids a second problem: taking a payment or recording a sale twice after connectivity returns.

Where the product fits in the actual workflow

NodeDR POS is documented as an open-source, offline-first retail POS that can run on a machine in the shop and serve phones, tablets and PCs on the same local network. The documented counter flow covers barcode scanning, GST billing, inventory, customer dues, receipts and reprints. Its repository also describes optional VPS hosting; that makes remote access easier but changes the failure path because the shop depends on the internet and the hosting provider. Payment authorizations, online-store integrations and other external services should not be assumed to work offline just because the core app can.

Walk through a realistic failure

Picture a customer scanning a product, choosing UPI and asking for a printed receipt while the shop’s ISP is down. These are three separate operations: local item lookup, external payment authorization and local printing. Test each one. The till should make the payment status obvious, and a sale should not be finalized as paid until the payment provider confirms it. If the shop uses manual sales temporarily, number every entry and assign one person to match the entries against later payment confirmations. This is especially useful when multiple staff share a counter during a busy period.

A practical continuity drill

Run this during a quiet period with the person who will make the decision during a real incident. Write down what you observe, what is unavailable, and how staff return to normal operation. A drill that ends with a clear owner and a measured recovery time is more useful than a checklist nobody has tried.

  • Test one normal sale from barcode scan through receipt with the WAN disconnected but local Wi-Fi active.
  • Test a different case: card or UPI authorization, online order and any hosted dashboard. Record what truly requires internet access.
  • Prepare a numbered manual-sales log and a rule for checking it against digital receipts after recovery.
  • Keep the router, till and local host on appropriate power backup, and test printer reconnection after a restart.

Questions to answer before relying on the setup

  • Does the till connect to a local host or a remote VPS?
  • What is the precise offline behaviour for each payment method?
  • Can the shop find the last confirmed sale after a router or server restart?
  • How will staff prevent duplicate sales during recovery?

Scope and limitations

Offline-first describes the local app workflow; card, UPI, online-store, hosted-VPS, and remote-access services may still need connectivity. Verify the exact payment and device setup before relying on it.

Product documentation to verify before deployment

NodeDR POS is described as an offline-first retail POS that runs on a shop machine and serves devices on the local network. Its documented counter workflow includes barcode scanning, GST billing, inventory, customer dues, and receipts. It can also be hosted on a VPS, which is a different availability and data-control choice.
