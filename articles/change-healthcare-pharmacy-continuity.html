FIELD NOTE 16 · KINETIRX

The Pharmacy Counter and the Claims Network Are Not the Same System

The 2024 Change Healthcare cyberattack disrupted claims and payment operations across the US health system. HHS described monitoring effects on access to care and concern for smaller practices.

By Raktim Ranjit · Updated 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

The incident, and the operational question

The lesson for independent pharmacies is to separate local work from external settlement. A local system can record a sale, but it cannot make a payer’s claims switch, eligibility service, or reimbursement network available when that external service is down. A good continuity checklist names each dependency and the reconciliation step.

What the primary record says

The US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services’ 6 March 2024 memorandum describes the Change Healthcare cyberattack as a disruption to claims and payment operations with downstream effects on health providers. The memo discusses steps intended to support continued access to care, especially for providers facing cash-flow pressure, and directs readers to federal updates as the response evolved. The event showed how a widely used intermediary can become a bottleneck across pharmacies, practices and payers. It did not mean every pharmacy’s local dispensing system stopped; claims processing, reimbursement, eligibility checks and the pharmacy’s counter workflow are related but distinct dependencies.

What this means for a working business

A pharmacy continuity plan should separate patient-facing care from back-office claims and reimbursement. Staff need to know what they can safely dispense, what payer checks are unavailable, how to record exceptions and who can authorize a fallback. Records captured during a disruption must be reconciled carefully when a network returns. This is not a reason to bypass clinical or payer rules; it is a reason to define approved procedures in advance with the pharmacy’s compliance and professional leads. Cash-flow planning matters too, because a prolonged claims disruption can create operating pressure even when the counter remains open.

Where the product fits in the actual workflow

KinetiRx documents local billing, inventory, patient records, due-khata and daily drawer reconciliation. Those features can support a pharmacy’s internal recordkeeping, but they do not replace a claims switch, insurer connection or regulatory guidance. A local system can preserve selected business workflows while an external payer network is impaired; it cannot make an external claim succeed or determine whether dispensing is authorized. Pharmacy owners should coordinate a downtime procedure with their pharmacists, payers and applicable authorities.

Walk through a realistic failure

A pharmacy counter can remain open while the external claims network is delayed, but staff must distinguish a local sale from a payer-confirmed claim. During a tabletop exercise, mark every step that needs eligibility lookup, claim submission or reimbursement. Ask the pharmacist which approved exception procedure applies and how the customer will be informed. Record only the data authorized by that procedure, keep the log secure, and assign a person to resubmit or reconcile claims later. A local billing system may organize shop activity, but it cannot make an external adjudication service respond or guarantee reimbursement.

A practical continuity drill

Run this during a quiet period with the person who will make the decision during a real incident. Write down what you observe, what is unavailable, and how staff return to normal operation. A drill that ends with a clear owner and a measured recovery time is more useful than a checklist nobody has tried.

  • Map the path from prescription intake through dispensing, eligibility check, claim submission and reimbursement.
  • Identify which steps have an approved downtime process and who may authorize it.
  • Use a controlled, numbered exception log; do not store more sensitive data than the approved process requires.
  • After recovery, reconcile rejected, pending and duplicate claims with the payer and dispensing record.

Questions to answer before relying on the setup

  • Which decisions remain clinical and which are administrative?
  • How does staff distinguish a saved claim from a confirmed claim?
  • Who tracks delayed reimbursements and vendor communications?
  • What data may be written down during downtime, and how is it protected?

Scope and limitations

Do not describe a local POS as a cure for a national claims outage. Confirm which transactions can be recorded offline and how they will be reconciled with outside networks.

Product documentation to verify before deployment

KinetiRx documents billing, mixed payment modes, a due-khata ledger, order tracking, and drawer reconciliation. These can help organize local pharmacy activity, but the product is not a replacement for insurer claims infrastructure or a guarantee of payer connectivity.
