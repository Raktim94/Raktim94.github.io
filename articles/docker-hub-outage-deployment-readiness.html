The Outage After the Outage: Can You Still Rebuild Your Software?

Docker reported that its October 2025 services were affected by the AWS US-EAST-1 event. It shows why recovery depends on deployment artifacts as well as running servers.

By Raktim Ranjit · 27 October 2026 · 5 min read

The incident, and the question it raises

A service can keep running while its image registry, build system, or deployment control plane is unavailable. Later, a failed host or rollback can turn that secondary dependency into the next outage. A realistic continuity plan asks whether you have pinned images, a copy of required artifacts, and a written path to rebuild.

Where NodeDR POS fits

NodeDR POS publishes Docker-based installation paths and documents an updater that swaps app containers without touching the data volume. Operators should understand how their own registry access, image tags, configuration, and backups behave before an incident; keeping a recovery checklist is part of self-hosting.

A clear boundary

The project does not claim that every user has a private image mirror or air-gapped rebuild. The operator must confirm the exact deployed version and make backups independently.
